Default vs strongest models: what changed?

I asked the same two questions a leader might ask an adviser. They went to three AIs: Grok Bot, ChatGPT and Claude. Claude and ChatGPT each answered twice, on an everyday model and on a strongest model: Claude Sonnet 5.5 (default), Claude Opus 5.5 (strongest), ChatGPT GPT-6.1-Sol (default) and ChatGPT GPT-6-Astra at high reasoning (strongest). Grok Bot gave one answer. No version number is published for it, so none is shown.

The answers were generated on 11 Oct 2026.

The questions were a Singapore bank chief executive’s priority for the next 90 days on AI agents, and how much of the effort should go into bottom-up AI, where teams try things for themselves.

Orange means the stronger model changed the answer. Green means it gave the same answer. The dark column is Grok Bot. It has one answer, so it is not marked orange or green.

What stayed the same

ChatGPT GPT-6.1-Sol (default) and ChatGPT GPT-6-Astra at high reasoning (strongest) gave almost the same answers, and both stayed on “start small.” Bottom-up work still clusters around 30% of the effort. Grok Bot puts it at 30–40%, paid mostly in staff time. In the Claude and ChatGPT answers, one person stays accountable, and anything that touches money or customers needs human approval.

What changed

Claude Opus 5.5 (strongest) moved away from Claude Sonnet 5.5 (default). It left “list every agent in use first,” a control-first stance, and said “pilot one or two back-office jobs, with controls” — prove it. On bottom-up investment it became more specific: set aside 5–10% of a team’s week, count the ideas other teams adopt, and each quarter decide whether to scale or stop. That is a move toward the view Grok Bot already held.

How Grok Bot compared

On both questions, Claude Opus 5.5 (strongest) moved toward the view Grok Bot already held: pilot a real process and prove it, and count the ideas other teams adopt. ChatGPT GPT-6.1-Sol (default) and ChatGPT GPT-6-Astra at high reasoning (strongest) stayed on “start small.” Grok Bot is the only one that put people first: staff ownership, customer satisfaction, recognition, and the risk that leaders don’t listen.

What this means for a leader

ChatGPT GPT-6.1-Sol (default) holds steady; Claude Opus 5.5 (strongest) sharpens the answer

ChatGPT GPT-6.1-Sol (default) and ChatGPT GPT-6-Astra at high reasoning (strongest) gave nearly the same advice. Claude Opus 5.5 (strongest) changed what Claude Sonnet 5.5 (default) said, from listing every agent to proving one or two jobs.

Which exact model you pick matters more for some AIs than for others. When the choice can change the recommendation, ask the same question of both versions before you treat the answer as settled.

On familiar questions, the best AIs now agree

Claude Opus 5.5 (strongest) and ChatGPT GPT-6-Astra at high reasoning (strongest) landed in the same place: start small, keep a human on money and customers, and put about 30% into bottom-up work.

The generic answer is now cheap. A leader’s edge is their own context and judgement: the jobs, the customers, and the risks that belong to them.

Differences get subtler, so ask harder, local questions

The remaining differences are matters of emphasis and detail. A familiar question now draws a consensus.

To see a real contrast, ask a harder question that only your organisation can answer: a local process, a real constraint, or a decision you are actually facing.
